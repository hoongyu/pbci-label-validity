# Gate G1.2 — attempt 2, descriptive variant (HARD STOP)
Date: 2026-08-05

Attempt 1 failed Part B and the failure was ruled **invalid**: band power came
from the ML preprocessing variant, while the published anomaly is a
**descriptive**-variant result (`dataset.md` §7.6, `pitfalls.md` #1). Attempt 1's
next action was to build the descriptive pipeline and re-test. That pipeline is
`src/preprocess/descriptive.py`; this is the re-test.

Part A is not re-run: RSME and behaviour do not depend on the EEG preprocessing
variant, and Part A passed with three F statistics matching to four significant
figures.

## Result: **INCONCLUSIVE — not a pass, and not the fork either**

The pilot covers **8 subjects, n=7 after listwise deletion**, against a
published n=29. The headline is that 6 of 7 criteria "pass" and one fails, in
all six parameter combinations. **That headline is misleading and should not be
reported.** See "Why the passes are not passes" below.

## What was run

24 subject-sessions (subjects 1–8 × 3 sessions) through the descriptive
pipeline. One file, `sub-01_ses-S1`, predates the multi-threshold refactor and
was **excluded** rather than merged — keeping it would have mixed two epoch
rejection criteria inside one ANOVA. It must be recomputed with `--force`.

Two parameters the references do not determine were not chosen; the entire test
ran under every value:

| Parameter | Values | Why undetermined |
|---|---|---|
| epoch rejection | `none`, `ptp150`, `ptp300` | `dataset.md` §7.5 gives the outcome (~16 epochs/task) but not the criterion |
| ROI channels | full montage, 10-channel subset | `dataset.md` does not name the ROI channels |

**Every criterion gives the same answer under all six combinations.** Neither
undetermined parameter drives any conclusion here, which is worth recording:
it removes two candidate explanations before they can be offered later.

## Criterion-by-criterion (ROI=full, reject=none; the others agree)

| Criterion | Result | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| N-Back: no difficulty effect on theta | OK | smallest p=0.179 (posterior, F=2.00) |
| N-Back: no difficulty effect on alpha | **FAIL** | smallest p=0.014 (frontal, F=6.17) |

MATB reproduces completely, including the counter-conventional **increase** in
posterior alpha. The pipeline is therefore sensitive: a null in N-Back cannot be
dismissed as a dead pipeline. That is the positive control and it holds.

## Why the passes are not passes

Every N-Back criterion is a **null** claim. A null at n=7 against a published
n=29 is weak by construction, and F falls with n for a fixed effect. Comparing p
values across the two attempts therefore invites precisely the wrong conclusion.
Partial η² is sample-size free (`descriptive_partb.partial_eta_sq`):

| N-Back measure | F (ML, n=29) | η²ₚ | F (desc, n=7) | η²ₚ | effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| theta frontal | 1.47 | 0.050 | 1.67 | **0.218** | **grew 4×** |
| theta central | 5.57 | 0.166 | 0.30 | 0.048 | shrank |
| theta posterior | 4.15 | 0.129 | 2.00 | **0.250** | **grew 2×** |
| alpha frontal | 15.76 | 0.360 | 6.17 | 0.507 | grew |
| alpha central | 26.83 | 0.489 | 5.67 | 0.486 | unchanged |
| alpha posterior | 28.62 | 0.505 | 3.24 | 0.350 | unchanged |

**Posterior theta passes the null criterion here while its effect size is twice
what it was when it failed at n=29.** Frontal theta passes with an effect four
times larger than before. These are underpowered nulls — absence of evidence —
and reporting them as confirmation of the anomaly would be wrong.

The switch to the descriptive variant did **not** shrink the N-Back alpha
effect: η²ₚ is 0.35–0.51 under both variants. Attempt 1's diagnosis (variant
mismatch) explains why attempt 1 was invalid, but it does not appear to explain
the N-Back alpha disagreement away. Only theta central genuinely improved.

Direction, for the record: N-Back alpha **decreases** monotonically with
difficulty (frontal −25.895 → −26.003 → −26.063), the conventional
desynchronisation. MATB posterior alpha **increases** (+0.441 over the same
span), the published anomalous direction, ~3× larger in magnitude.

## Correcting my own decision rule

The plan I stated before running the pilot was: *if the anomaly appears, extend
to all 29; if it does not, this is the fork.* **That rule is biased** — it gives
a confirming result more data and a disconfirming result less, so the fuller
sample only ever gets collected when it agrees with the hypothesis. I did not
notice this until the pilot came back disconfirming. Recorded in
`deviations.md`, with a symmetric replacement pre-committed before the remaining
subjects are run.

## Amendment, same day — the ML comparison was confounded with the sample

The effect-size table above compares the descriptive pilot (n=7) against the
attempt-1 ML run (n=29). That comparison confounds two things: the
preprocessing variant, and **which subjects**. Partial η² removes most of the
sample-size dependence of F, but it is not the fix I treated it as — the
estimator is biased upward at small n, and more importantly it says nothing
about sampling variation in *which* seven subjects were drawn.

`descriptive_partb.matched_n_ml` now re-fits the ML variant on exactly the
subjects the descriptive run covers, so the variant is the only difference:

| N-Back measure | η²ₚ ML n=29 | η²ₚ ML **same subjects** | η²ₚ desc | revised reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| theta frontal | 0.050 | 0.055 | 0.218 | **real**: 4× survives matched n |
| theta central | 0.166 | 0.160 | 0.048 | **real**: shrank |
| theta posterior | 0.129 | **0.238** | 0.250 | **artefact**: unchanged, not "2× larger" |
| alpha frontal | 0.360 | 0.322 | 0.507 | grew |
| alpha central | 0.489 | 0.284 | 0.486 | grew |
| alpha posterior | 0.505 | **0.262** | 0.350 | within noise, not "unchanged at 0.5" |

**Corrections to the section above.** Posterior theta was reported as passing
the null criterion "while its effect size is twice what it was" — it is not.
The ML variant on these same subjects gives 0.238 against the descriptive 0.250.
The doubling was a property of which subjects were drawn, not of the effect.
Frontal theta's 4× growth does survive matched n, so that claim stands, but for
a different reason than the one given.

The wider point is worse than the individual corrections. **Alpha posterior
under the unchanged ML pipeline moves from η²ₚ=0.505 at n=29 to 0.262 at n=8.**
Same data, same code, same measure — halved by subject sampling alone. Any
reading of this pilot that leans on an effect size is reading noise, mine
included.

## What the pilot does establish

One thing, and the underpowered-null caveat does not apply to it. Every N-Back
criterion is a null claim, so a **pass** is weak — that was the argument above
and it holds. But the N-Back alpha criterion does not pass. It **fails in all
six parameter combinations**, at n=7, where power is low. Rejecting a null
despite low power is evidence in the direction of rejection; low power is the
reason to distrust the *passes*, not the failure.

Set against the ML variant's p=2.7e-9 for the same measure at n=29, the N-Back
alpha difficulty effect is the one result here that is not fragile.

This does **not** trigger the fork, and does not license skipping the remaining
subjects. The pre-committed rule in `deviations.md` is applied at n≈29, on the
primary combination (ROI=full, reject=none), or not at all. Recording it here so
that if the n≈29 result agrees, it is visible that I expected it to — and if it
disagrees, that expectation is on the record too.
