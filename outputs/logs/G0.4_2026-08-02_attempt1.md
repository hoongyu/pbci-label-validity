## Two findings that matter more than the headline

### 1. ICA did not converge in 90% of sessions

`ica_n_iter` was instrumented partway through the sweep and recorded for 70 of
87 sessions:

| Iterations | Sessions |
|---|---|
| < 500 (converged) | 7 |
| **500 (cap reached)** | **63 (90%)** |

`max_iter='auto'` resolves to 500 for extended Infomax, so a session reporting
500 stopped at the cap rather than at a solution. Converged sessions averaged
7.5 min; capped ones 65 min — non-convergence is what made the sweep a
multi-day job.

**This is a quality question, not only a cost one.** ICLabel classifies
whatever components the decomposition produces, and those classifications
decide what is subtracted from the data. For 90% of the dataset that
decomposition is unconverged.

Mitigating evidence, and the reason this is logged rather than acted on: the
baseline reproduces anyway, on both tasks, on all three directional criteria,
and with a session profile matching to 1.4 pp. Whatever ICA is doing, it is
doing what the published pipeline did closely enough to land inside a ±3 pp
window on five independent criteria. The published pipeline used MATLAB ICLabel
and very likely hit its own iteration limits.

**Do not "fix" this now.** Raising `max_iter` or lowering `n_components` after
G0.4 has passed would change every downstream number for no established
benefit, and would be a deviation made after seeing a result. If it is
revisited, it belongs before P2 preprocessing, as a preregistered decision with
the convergence rate reported.

### 2. The between-subject variance alarm was mine, and it was wrong

At the n = 5 checkpoint I flagged that my sample SD (5.29 MATB / 3.75 N-Back)
was less than half the published 12.50 / 12.99, and warned this could weaken
H2 for a methodological reason, since H2 depends on between-subject
heterogeneity in accuracy.

At n = 29 the per-subject SD is 6.92 / 6.85 — still roughly half, and χ²
against the published values gives p < 0.001. Taken at face value that looked
like confirmation.

It is not. The published ± is a **cell-level** SD, not a between-subject one:

| Task | per-subject SD | per-cell SD | published ± |
|---|---|---|---|
| MATB | 6.92 | **11.51** | 12.50 (ratio 0.92) |
| N-Back | 6.85 | **11.51** | 12.99 (ratio 0.89) |

Compared like-for-like the dispersion reproduces to within ~10%. Averaging each
subject's 6 cells necessarily shrinks the SD; I had been comparing that
shrunken quantity against an unshrunken one.

Two corrections follow. The pipeline does not compress between-subject
variance. And the concern was misdirected regardless: H2 is a correlation over
per-subject **LOSO** accuracy (`analysis.md` §6), a P2 quantity, not the
within-subject CV accuracy measured here.

## Open, for the mentor

Six independent signs that the reference documents describe an earlier COG-BCI
revision than the one currently on Zenodo:

1. `TP9` absent; the ECG channel is `ECG1`
2. extraction is 35.89 GB, not `dataset.md` §1's ~50 GB
3. 27 of 29 archives carry an undocumented extra directory level
4. "2 SD" is irreconcilable with ~0.34 channels/task
5. N-Back trial pacing is 2.516 s in session 1 against a documented 2.0 s
   (2.008 s in session 3), and per-session N-Back accuracy tracks pacing at
   Spearman r = 0.946
6. `gates.md` §G0.2's "assert TP9 is identified as ECG" cannot be satisfied as
   worded

Item 5 is the strongest: a measurable deviation in an experimental parameter,
not a packaging or documentation slip. `pitfalls.md` endorses contacting the
dataset authors on reproducibility questions.
