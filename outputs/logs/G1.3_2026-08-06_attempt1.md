## Three findings that are not in the checklist

**1. D = 0 is unattainable, and everything read against 0 inherits the offset.**
τ-b's denominator penalises a mismatch in *tie structure*, not only in ordering.
Nominal difficulty always has three ties of three. A subject perfectly ordered
across difficulty levels but varying within a level scores τ_b = 0.866, i.e.
**D = 0.134, not 0**. Because `analysis.md` §1 deliberately keeps session as a
replicate rather than averaging it away, every real subject has within-level
variation, so the entire observed D distribution is shifted up off zero.

This is visible in the data: MATB's minimum D_beh is exactly 0.134, the
untied floor, and MATB's minimum D_subj is 0.106 (slightly lower because that
subject has some RSME ties). Any threshold, effect size or "perfect tracking"
reference compared against 0 is biased by roughly this amount. Pinned by
`test_d_zero_is_unreachable_once_cells_differ_within_a_difficulty`.

**2. The per-subject CIs are very wide.** Median CI width is **0.773** for
N-Back D_subj on a [0, 2] scale — about 39% of the full range — and 0.607 for
MATB. `analysis.md` §1 anticipated this ("nine points is thin for a rank
statistic") and requires the uncertainty to be propagated into H2 rather than
treating each D as a point estimate. That requirement is now quantified: at
this width, any H2 analysis using per-subject D as a fixed covariate is
overstating its precision substantially.

**3. D_subj and D_beh do not agree.**

| Task | Spearman ρ | p |
|---|---|---|
| N-Back | **−0.153** | 0.429 |
| MATB | +0.311 | 0.101 |

Neither is significant, and the N-Back sign is *negative*. `analysis.md` §1 is
explicit that this case is a finding, not a defect: *"If they disagree, that
disagreement is itself a finding and must be reported, not resolved by picking
the more convenient one."*

Subjective divergence and behavioural divergence are measuring different
things. That matters directly for the project, because D_subj is the PRIMARY
metric and the relabelling in Study 2 rests on it. It also compounds the G1.2
result: the label's validity is now questionable against EEG *and* the two
non-EEG criteria for label validity do not agree with each other.
