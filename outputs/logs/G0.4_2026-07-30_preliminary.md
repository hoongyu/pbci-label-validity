### Is the MATB overshoot evidence of a problem?

Not at this n. The window is a ±3 pp tolerance around a mean of 29 subjects;
the sampling error of a 5-subject mean is far larger. Testing against the
**published between-subject SD** (12.50 for MATB, 12.99 for N-Back) rather than
against my own 5-subject sample SD:

| Task | Observed | Expected range for an n=5 mean | z | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MATB | 76.92% | [58.44, 80.36] | +1.35 | 0.179 |
| N-Back | 64.05% | [53.58, 76.36] | −0.16 | 0.874 |

Both consistent. An earlier one-sample t-test using my own sample SD returned
p = 0.034 for MATB and is **not** the right comparison — it treats five
subjects' spread as the population spread.

### One thing to watch, because it bears on the project's thesis

My 5-subject sample SDs are **5.29 (MATB) and 3.75 (N-Back)** against published
12.50 and 12.99. For MATB that is χ²₄ = 0.72, p ≈ 0.051 in the low tail — an
unusually homogeneous sample if the true SD is 12.50.

With n = 5 this may be chance. But if it persists as subjects are added, it
would mean **the pipeline compresses between-subject variance**, and that is
not a cosmetic issue: the entire project rests on between-subject heterogeneity
in decoding accuracy (H2 correlates per-subject divergence with per-subject
LOSO accuracy, `analysis.md` §6). A pipeline that flattens that variance would
weaken H2 for a purely methodological reason. Recheck at n = 29.

Mine rises monotonically; the published peaks at session 2. Part of this is
understood: N-Back session 1 scores 51.68% because of trial pacing (median ITI
2.516 s in session 1 against 2.008 s in session 3, against a documented 2.0 s
— see `deviations.md` and the `task_windows` docstring), and per-session N-Back
accuracy tracks pacing at Spearman r = 0.946, p = 0.0001.

That does **not** explain MATB, which is 59 epochs in every session and still
climbs 71.09 → 78.55 → 81.13. Unresolved. Re-evaluate at n = 29 before
investigating, since a 5-subject session profile is noisy.

## What was ruled out getting here

- **Session pooling.** Costs ~13 pp on both tasks (MATB 75.15 → 62.20, N-Back
  58.41 → 47.20). Per-session scoring is correct, as the published F(2,56)
  session effect implies.
- **Inverted difficulty axis** from the descending notebook numbering. Cannot
  affect accuracy — relabelling is a permutation and 3-class accuracy is
  invariant to it.
- **Rest contamination of N-Back epochs.** Trials are contiguous; task windows
  cover 88–96% either way. The session difference is pacing, not rest.
- **An N-Back-specific pipeline fault.** There is none: N-Back reproduces to
  within 0.9 pp once all three sessions are scored. The apparent 13 pp deficit
  was an artifact of scoring session 1 alone.
