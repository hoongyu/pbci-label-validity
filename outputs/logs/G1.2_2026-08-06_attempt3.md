## The pipeline is not dead — MATB is a strong positive control

Every MATB criterion reproduces in the primary combination, including the
counter-conventional finding the published work reports: **posterior alpha
*increases* with difficulty** (+0.268 in subject-centred log power, monotonic).
Frontal and posterior theta reach F=57 and F=119. A null in N-Back therefore
cannot be dismissed as an insensitive pipeline; the pipeline detects large
effects in the other task with the same code, the same subjects and the same
session.

## What actually diverges, and what replicates

Subject-centred log power, ROI=full, reject=none:

| Task | Band | ROI | easy | med | hard | shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| N-Back | alpha | frontal | +0.048 | +0.020 | −0.068 | **monotonic ↓** (−0.116) |
| N-Back | alpha | central | +0.047 | +0.025 | −0.072 | **monotonic ↓** (−0.118) |
| N-Back | alpha | posterior | +0.056 | +0.030 | −0.085 | **monotonic ↓** (−0.141) |
| N-Back | theta | frontal | −0.012 | −0.007 | +0.019 | ↑ (+0.031) |
| N-Back | theta | central | +0.005 | −0.004 | −0.001 | non-monotonic (−0.006) |
| N-Back | theta | posterior | +0.018 | +0.004 | −0.022 | ↓ (−0.040) |
| MATB | alpha | posterior | −0.141 | +0.014 | +0.127 | **monotonic ↑** (+0.268) |

**The published N-Back theta null replicates.** Theta is flat, and what movement
there is does not survive as an effect (p=0.206 primary; non-significant in 5 of
6 combinations). The divergence is narrow and specific: **N-Back alpha**.

And it is not a marginal miss. N-Back alpha falls monotonically with load across
all three ROIs — the conventional alpha desynchronisation, in the textbook
direction, with an effect size (η²ₚ=0.36 posterior) that is large.

## The variant mismatch was real but is not the explanation

Attempt 1 failed Part B on ML-variant power and I ruled that failure invalid,
because the published anomaly is a descriptive-variant result. That reasoning
was correct and the descriptive variant *did* shrink the effects — but not to
zero, and not close to it:

| N-Back | η²ₚ ML (n=29) | η²ₚ descriptive (n=29) | |
|---|---|---|---|
| alpha frontal | 0.360 | 0.248 | shrank ~⅓, still large |
| alpha central | 0.489 | 0.342 | shrank ~⅓, still large |
| alpha posterior | 0.505 | 0.362 | shrank ~⅓, still large |
| theta frontal | 0.050 | 0.023 | both null |
| theta central | 0.166 | 0.004 | **now null** |
| theta posterior | 0.129 | 0.055 | **now null** |

Switching variants moved theta from "partly significant" to "cleanly null",
which is exactly the correction attempt 1 predicted and is why re-running was
right. It did not move alpha out of significance. `matched_n_ml` now equals the
n=29 ML column, as it must at full sample — a consistency check on the code.

## What this means, and what I am not deciding

The published claim is that in N-Back, subjective load, behaviour and cardiac
measures track difficulty while theta and alpha do not. Half of that replicates
cleanly (theta). The alpha half does not: alpha tracks difficulty strongly and
in the conventional direction.

One observation to put in front of the mentor rather than act on. Alpha moves in
**opposite directions in the two tasks** — down with load in N-Back (−0.141
posterior), up with load in MATB (+0.268 posterior). Both are called "workload"
manipulations. A band that reverses sign between two workload tasks is not
behaving like a workload index, which is close to the question the project
exists to ask — but reaching that conclusion through a route the PRD did not
anticipate is exactly the situation `gates.md` reserves for the mentor.

`gates.md` §G1.2 is a hard stop and says the fork is re-planned, not routed
around. I am not re-specifying the test, dropping the alpha criterion, or
reframing the project to accommodate the result.
